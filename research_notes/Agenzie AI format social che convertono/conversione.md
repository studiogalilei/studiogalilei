# Conversion mechanics: from social content to paying clients (B2B software/AI/marketing agencies)

Method note: the research ran on 2026-11-21. Most numbers below come from search-engine summaries of primary pages. Direct fetches of refinelabs.com, letterdrop.com, sparktoro.com and edelman.com failed (those hosts are blocked by this session's network allowlist), so exact wording should be checked against the originals before publication. Labels used: **[VERIFIED-PRIMARY]** = the publisher's own press release or report page shows up in the results; **[SECONDARY]** = trade press or a blog restating a report; **[VENDOR]** = the publisher sells the tool being measured; **[SELF-REPORTED]** = a founder or creator claim with no audit.

## 1. Mechanics: which lead-capture mechanics convert, and at what rates

### Takeaway
No independent benchmark compares comment-to-DM, lead magnets, audits, webinars, communities and warm outbound head to head for agencies. The figures that exist are vendor numbers measured in different ways. Read them this way: (a) automated comment-to-DM delivery is vendor-reported at about 12-20% conversion of qualified leads; (b) webinars convert about 57-60% of registrants into attendees (ON24); (c) outreach to people who engaged with content ("warm" or signal-based outbound) gets reply rates several times higher than cold outreach (vendor data: 15-45% vs under 1-5%). I found no reliable public rate for "free audit/teardown", Skool communities or retargeting video viewers in an agency context.

### Cited Findings
**Comment-to-DM keyword automation (ManyChat and similar)**
- ManyChat says qualified leads captured through DM automation convert at **12-20%**, against an average of about **10%** for landing pages, and calls it "the highest reported conversion rate range we found in our research". ManyChat also admits there is "no single average conversion rate across all marketing channels". [VENDOR] — [ManyChat, Conversion rates by marketing channel](https://manychat.com/blog/conversion-rates-by-marketing-channel/)
- ManyChat case study (Jenna Kutcher, B2C creator, run with an agency partner): **2,650 emails** collected, **924 purchases**, presented as a "35% purchase conversion rate". This is a single curated case and B2C. [VENDOR] — [ManyChat blog / success stories](https://manychat.com/blog/how-course-creators-use-instagram-dm-automation/)
- Mindvalley case: "522% more sign-ups" after moving to Instagram DM automation. [VENDOR, B2C] — [ManyChat, Mindvalley](https://manychat.com/blog/success-stories/mindvalley/)
- Planoly: there is "no standard benchmark" for automated DMs because vendors define conversion differently, and there is no independent data by account size. Its advice is to benchmark against your own link-in-bio results. — [Planoly, Do automated Instagram DMs actually work?](https://www.planoly.com/blog/do-automated-instagram-dms-work)
- Vendor data (Creator Lane, cited by Planoly): **92.25%** delivery rate for automated DMs. This measures delivery, not conversion. [VENDOR] — [Planoly](https://www.planoly.com/blog/do-automated-instagram-dms-work)

**Webinars / live**
- ON24 2025 Webinar Benchmarks (2024 platform data, "millions of data points"): average registration-to-attendee conversion of **57%**. — [ON24 2025 Webinar Benchmarks Report](https://www.on24.com/resources/asset/webinar-benchmarks-report) [VENDOR, large platform dataset]
- ON24 figures on 2025 data: registration-to-attendee conversion reaches **60%**; about **239 attendees** per webinar on average; average engagement time **49 minutes**; CTAs with personalization convert **48% more** than CTAs without. — [ON24, key takeaways](https://www.on24.com/blog/key-takeaways-from-the-webinar-benchmarks-report/); [MarketingProfs chart](https://www.marketingprofs.com/charts/2025/52917/b2b-webinar-benchmarks-conversion-attendance-personalization?e=4); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/403429/working-webinars-personalization-can-lead-to-reco.html)
- Other sources put typical attendance at 40-50% of registrants (not ON24, so not comparable). — [WaveCNCT webinar statistics](https://wavecnct.com/blogs/news/webinar-statistics) [SECONDARY]

**Warm outbound to people who engaged with content (post engagers, profile viewers)**
- Valley (30M+ messages, 1,000+ LinkedIn accounts): warm, signal-based outreach gets **15-45%** replies depending on segment, against **under 1%** for cold outreach without context. Post engagers are named among the top warm segments. [VENDOR] — [Valley, LinkedIn reply rates 2026](https://www.joinvalley.co/blog/linkedin-reply-rate-benchmarks-2026)
- Letterdrop: outreach to engaged accounts reached reply rates **above 30%**, against the typical **under 5%** for standard connection requests and InMails. Letterdrop itself warns the data "may in no way represent" the wider B2B SaaS market. [VENDOR, single case] — [Letterdrop study](https://letterdrop.com/blog/study-effect-of-linkedin-activity-on-outbound); [Letterdrop, engaged prospects](https://letterdrop.com/blog/impact-of-engaged-prospects-linkedin)
- PhantomBuster describes "social warming" (engaging with a prospect before connecting) as a lever that lifts LinkedIn acceptance rates. — [PhantomBuster, warm vs cold outreach](https://phantombuster.com/blog/outbound-sales/warm-outreach-vs-cold-outreach/) [VENDOR]
- HeyReach playbook: "turn LinkedIn familiarity into a 50% acceptance rate". [VENDOR] — [HeyReach](https://www.heyreach.io/outbound-playbooks/turn-linkedin-familiarity-into-a-50-acceptance-rate)

**Lead magnets / audits / Skool / retargeting**
- Generic B2B agency KPIs: lead conversion **2-15%**, CAC **$500-2K**. No methodology given. [SECONDARY/consultancy] — [Predictable Profits](https://predictableprofits.com/top-7-kpi-benchmarks-for-b2b-agencies/)
- The searches turned up no rate for n8n/Make templates, custom GPTs, free audits or Skool communities used as an agency acquisition channel. There is anecdotal evidence only, e.g. Skool threads where people ask whether YouTube really brings inbound for AI automation services. — [Skool, Learn AI thread](https://www.skool.com/learn-ai/need-honest-feedback-from-people-getting-real-inbound-from-youtube-for-ai-automation-services) [SELF-REPORTED/anecdotal]

### Inferences
- The highest-converting mechanics are those that (1) move the conversation into a private 1:1 channel (DM, call) and (2) target people who have already shown interest (engagers, webinar registrants). This fits the Edelman data (section 2) that sharing happens 89% in private channels.
- For an AI agency, the "comment keyword → DM with n8n template → follow-up asking a qualifying question → call" chain combines two of the strongest levers (DM automation and warm outbound). The step-by-step rates are still not documented, so the agency should measure them itself.
- The ManyChat 12-20% figure is a ceiling from a vendor and from B2C creators. In high-ticket B2B services, conversion to a paid contract will be much lower. The numbers worth tracking are DM→call and call→close.

### Gaps
- No independent study of comment-to-DM in B2B (LinkedIn has no native comment-to-DM API comparable to Instagram's; on LinkedIn the mechanic is manual or semi-automated).
- No data on retargeting LinkedIn/Meta video viewers for agencies (LinkedIn retargeting audience rates were not found in this round).
- No public conversion rates for "free audit/teardown" or for Skool communities as a funnel into high-ticket services.

## 2. Dark social and attribution

### Takeaway
B2B buyers do most of their research before contacting a vendor and do it in channels that tracking software does not see. The 6sense 2025 report: buyers have ranked preferred vendors before first contact in 94% of cases. Refine Labs: software attribution misses about 90% of the influence of "dark social" channels, which self-reported attribution brings to light. Edelman-LinkedIn 2026: 89% of those who share thought leadership do so through private channels.

### Cited Findings
**Refine Labs / self-reported attribution (HDYHAU, "How did you hear about us?")**
- 12-month Refine Labs study: **620 declared-intent conversions**, **$21.5M** of closed-won ARR, comparing software attribution with self-reported attribution. It finds a **90% measurement gap**, concentrated in "dark social" channels (social media, podcasts, word of mouth, communities). Date of the study not confirmed (it is probably 2022-2023: **flag as older data**). — [Refine Labs, Hybrid attribution framework](https://www.refinelabs.com/blog/hybrid-attribution-framework); [Refine Labs news](https://www.refinelabs.com/news/hybrid-attribution-framework)
- In the same dataset the podcast is credited with **53% of revenue ($11.4M closed-won)** in self-reported attribution and **0%** in software attribution. — [Refine Labs](https://www.refinelabs.com/blog/hybrid-attribution-framework) [SECONDARY via search summary]
- Secondary summaries of the same study: **85%** of converted customers self-reported a dark-social touchpoint; software attributed **78%** to web search. The summaries are internally inconsistent (one says customers self-report search at 12%). — [Leadgen Economy, Dark funnel 2026](https://www.leadgen-economy.com/blog/dark-funnel-self-reported-attribution-incrementality-2026/) [SECONDARY, inconsistent]
- A LinkedIn post by the Refine Labs founder (Chris Walker) cites social media at **41% of closed-won revenue** per self-reported attribution and claims software under-reports those channels by more than **95%**. This framing is less documented than the study. — reported in the [search results on Refine Labs](https://www.refinelabs.com/blog/attribution-mirage) [SELF-REPORTED]
- Methodological limits of HDYHAU (recall bias, "last-memory" bias): — [Recast, HDYHAU limitations](https://getrecast.com/hdyhau/); practical guides: [Blend B2B](https://blendb2b.com/blog/self-reported-attribution), [Outbrain](https://www.outbrain.com/blog/?p=47652)
- Databox has original research on the "disappearing buyer trail" (beyond attribution). — [Databox](https://databox.com/beyond-attribution-the-disappearing-buyer-trail) (not read in detail)

**Buyer journey before contact (6sense, Gartner)**
- 6sense Buyer Experience Report **2025** (Nov 2025): the research/vendor split moved from about **70/30 to 60/40**; **94%** of buying groups had ranked preferred vendors before first contact; buying cycle about **10 months** (vs about 11 in 2024). — [BusinessWire, 12 Nov 2025](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251112018032/en/) [VERIFIED-PRIMARY press release]
- A secondary summary of the 2025 report: first contact at **61%** of the journey (vs **69%** before). Not confirmed in the press release. — [CustomerThink](https://customerthink.com/research-round-up-6sense-study-provides-critical-insights-on-b2b-buyer-behavior/) [SECONDARY]
- 6sense **2024**: buyers were about **70%** through the journey before engaging sellers and initiated first contact in **more than 80%** of cases. — [DemandGen Report](https://www.demandgenreport.com/industry-news/80-of-b2b-buyers-initiate-first-contact-once-theyre-70-through-their-buying-journey/48394/); [BusinessWire 2024](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20241009142556/en/6sense-Launches-2024-Buyer-Experience-Report-Unveiling-Global-B2B-Buyer-Trends)
- Gartner (June 2025, 632 B2B buyers, fieldwork Aug-Sep 2024): **61%** prefer a rep-free buying experience; **73%** actively avoid vendors that send irrelevant outreach; **69%** find inconsistencies between the website and what sellers say. Buyers still want a seller for tasks that need "contextual intelligence" (fit with their needs). — [Gartner press release 25 Jun 2025](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-sales-survey-finds-61-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-buying-experience) [VERIFIED-PRIMARY]
- Gartner (March 2026, 646 buyers, fieldwork Aug-Sep 2025): rep-free preference rises to **67%**; **45%** used AI in a recent purchase. — [Gartner press release 9 Mar 2026](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-03-09-gartner-sales-survey-finds-67-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-experience) [VERIFIED-PRIMARY]
- The often-cited "17% of time spent with suppliers" (Gartner) did not turn up in 2025-2026 sources. It is an older figure (about 2019); **flag it, do not use it as current**.

**LinkedIn B2B Institute 95:5 rule**
- John Dawes (Ehrenberg-Bass) for the LinkedIn B2B Institute (published 2021, **older data**): about **5%** of B2B buyers are in-market at any given moment, **95%** are out-of-market. The implication is to invest in brand/memory for future buying situations, not only lead gen. — [LinkedIn B2B Institute, 95-5 rule](https://business.linkedin.com/it/it/advertise/resources/b2b-institute/b2b-research/trends/95-5-rule); [Marketing Week](https://www.marketingweek.com/peter-weinberg-jon-lombardo-95-5-rule); [Dreamdata](https://dreamdata.io/blog/the-95-5-rule-john-dawes)
- Caveats: Dawes says the percentages are not meant to be taken literally. LinkedIn's CMO (2023) talked about "99:1" for some categories and said the rule is not a 95:5 budget split; best practice is around 50:50 or 60:40 brand:activation. Intent-data vendors argue more buyers are in-market than 5%. — [mi-3](https://mi-3.com.au/10-05-2023/linkedin-global-cmo-something-something-ai-brand); [CustomerThink](https://customerthink.com/what-the-955-rule-means-for-b2b-marketing/)

**Buying committees and hidden buyers (Edelman-LinkedIn)**
- **2025** report (7th edition, nearly **2,000** respondents, visible and hidden decision-makers): **more than 40%** of deals stall because of internal misalignment in the buying group; **79%** are more likely to champion a vendor in an RFP if it consistently publishes quality thought leadership; **85%** cite understanding of their specific challenges as a decisive factor; **53%** say that with strong thought leadership brand recognition matters less; **91%** of hidden buyers say strong thought leadership makes them more receptive to outreach (another Edelman page says 95%, which looks like a discrepancy). — [Edelman 2025 report](https://www.edelman.com/expertise/business-marketing/2025-b2b-thought-leadership-report); [DemandGen Report](https://www.demandgenreport.com/industry-news/news-brief/marketers-need-to-gear-content-to-hidden-b2b-buyers-edelman-linkedin/49901/); [Edelman, hidden buyer](https://www.edelman.com/insights/hidden-buyer-b2b) [SECONDARY via search; PDF not read]
- **2026** report (8th edition, released Nov 2026, **more than 1,100** executives, 7 countries): **84%** used AI to summarize thought leadership during vendor evaluation; **82%** say thought leadership is becoming more valuable as AI becomes a common source of answers; **89%** of those who shared thought leadership did so through **private channels** (vs **67%** through social/public channels); **44%** say poor or absent thought leadership contributed to removing a vendor from consideration. — [Edelman 2026 report](https://www.edelman.com/expertise/Business-Marketing/2026-b2b-thought-leadership-report); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/82-of-b2b-buyers-value-thought-leadership-more-as-ai-spreads-edelman-finds/); [ContentGrip](https://www.contentgrip.com/edelman-ai-thought-leadership-buyers/); [O'Dwyer's, 13 Nov 2026](https://www.odwyerpr.com/story/public/25611/2026-11-13/thought-leadership-counts-b2b-buying-decisions.html)
- **2024** report (**3,484** executives, fieldwork Dec 2023, focus on out-of-market buyers). Don't mix it with the 2025/2026 numbers. — [Edelman 2024 PDF](https://www.edelman.com/sites/g/files/aatuss191/files/2024-02/_2024%20Edelman-LinkedIn%20B2B%20Thought%20Leadership%20Impact%20Report%20Final.pdf); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/linkedin-edelman-report-thought-leadership-content-influences-purchase-decisions/753023)

### Inferences
- An agency should add a mandatory open-text HDYHAU field to the call-booking form and record the answer in its CRM. It is the only practical way to measure the effect of LinkedIn/YouTube content, given that UTM tracking misses most of the influence.
- Because 89% of sharing is private, the content most worth producing is content a person can forward internally (one-pagers, case studies with numbers, frameworks). It is aimed at "hidden buyers" (CFO, IT, ops) as well as the champion.
- With 84% of buyers summarizing content with AI, structured content with explicit numbers is likely to "survive" the summary better. This is an inference, not measured.

### Gaps
- The Refine Labs study's date and exact numbers were not checked on the primary source (host blocked). No more recent replication (2024-2026) of a self-reported vs software study was found.
- No dark-social data specific to agencies or professional services (all of the above is about B2B SaaS/enterprise).

## 3. Benchmarks: lead → call → close, CAC, sales cycle

### Takeaway
No solid, agency-specific funnel benchmark by lead source exists in the public sources found. The available figures (vendor blogs) show referral/inbound with a "demo request" closing much better than cold. Ranges: inbound demo request **30-45%** win rate, content download **15-25%**, referral **50-70%**, cold **10-20%**. They contradict each other and are methodologically weak. For agencies, referral is still the main source of new business.

### Cited Findings
- Win rates by source (2026, vendor blog): inbound demo request **30-45%**, inbound content download **15-25%**. — [Landbase, win rate benchmarks 2026](https://www.landbase.com/blog/win-rate-benchmarks-industry-deal-size-2026) [VENDOR]
- Referral: sales-call close rate **25.56%** vs **9.38%** for cold calling (one source); win rate **50-70%** for referred opportunities vs **10-20%** for cold (another source). Different metrics, not comparable. — [GrowSurf, B2B referral stats](https://growsurf.com/statistics/b2b-referral-marketing-statistics/); [Martech Zone, B2B referral benchmarks](https://martech.zone/b2b-referral-benchmarks/) [SECONDARY]
- Contradictory figure: one source claims outbound-sourced leads convert **34% more** than inbound. — reported in [Martal, B2B sales benchmarks 2026](https://martal.ca/b2b-sales-benchmarks) [VENDOR outbound agency, potential conflict of interest]
- HubSpot survey data (cited secondhand): average win rate **21%**, average close rate **29%**. — [Cirrus Insight](https://www.cirrusinsight.com/blog/sales-conversion-rate-benchmarks) [SECONDARY]
- High-ticket B2B services: typical close/win rate **20-30%**, consultative and referral-heavy sales. — [Prospeo, average sales close rate](https://prospeo.io/s/average-sales-close-rate) [VENDOR]
- Digital agencies: client referrals remain the main source of new business (SparkToro surveys on the state of digital agencies, 2024 and the follow-up). — [SparkToro 2024](https://sparktoro.com/blog/the-state-of-digital-agencies-in-2024-client-referrals-drive-agency-new-business-but-is-it-enough-and-are-sales-pipelines-a-cause-for-concern); [SparkToro follow-up](https://sparktoro.com/blog/digital-agency-new-business-still-a-concern-referrals-still-rule-for-lead-gen-and-are-sales-pipelines-finally-recovering/) (exact percentages not extracted: host blocked)
- Sales cycle: 6sense 2025 puts the average B2B buying cycle at about **10 months** (enterprise/tech buyers, not agencies). — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251112018032/en/)
- Generic agency KPIs: lead conversion 2-15%, CAC $500-2K, churn <5%. — [Predictable Profits](https://predictableprofits.com/top-7-kpi-benchmarks-for-b2b-agencies/) [consultancy, no methodology]
- HubSpot State of Marketing 2026 (1,500+ marketers): short-form video is the format with the highest ROI, **48.6-49%** of marketers put it in their top 3 (long-form video 29%, live 25%); **104%** more marketers name short-form video as their most valuable channel vs 2024; lead-to-customer conversion is the second most important KPI; **93.8%** say lead quality improved. — [HubSpot blog, 2026 State of Marketing](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/inbound-marketing-data); [HubSpot marketing channels 2026](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/marketing-channels?EPU=false); [SlideShare report](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/2026-state-of-marketing-report-by-hubspot-e1a1/286809253) [VENDOR survey; figures 48.6 vs 49 slightly inconsistent]

### Inferences
- Reasonable working range for an AI agency with content-sourced inbound (a lead who books a call after following the founder): close rate on the call likely between the "content download" (15-25%) and "demo request/referral" (30-70%) bands, because the lead arrives pre-sold. **This is an inference, not measured data.**
- Content-led vs outbound CAC: no comparative public data for agencies. The inferred structural difference is that content CAC is mostly founder time (fixed cost, compounding), while outbound CAC is variable (tools, domains, SDRs).

### Gaps
- No agency-specific benchmark for "social-sourced lead → call → close" from a reliable source.
- No comparative content-led vs outbound CAC data for agencies.
- No data on the sales cycle of content-sourced leads vs other sources for agencies.
- The AgencyAnalytics 2025 Marketing Agency Benchmarks report was mentioned but not found or read.

## 4. Content + outbound: does content raise reply rates?

### Takeaway
There are vendor and anecdotal data showing outreach performs much better when the prospect knows the sender (engagers, active profile), but no controlled study with a holdout. The best "baselines" for cold: Instantly 3.43% average reply rate for cold email; Expandi 5.1% cold email and 10.3% LinkedIn DM. The warm figures run 15-45%.

### Cited Findings
- Instantly Cold Email Benchmark Report 2026 (2025 sending data): average reply rate **3.43%**; top quartile about **5.5%**; top 10% about **10.7%**; **58%** of replies come from the first email; 4-7 steps is the "sweet spot"; 4-6 weeks of warmup for new domains. [VENDOR] — [Instantly, Cold Email Benchmark Report 2026](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026)
- Other cold baselines: Belkins about **5.8%** (managed campaigns); Smartlead **0.74%** typical reply rate on its sends. [VENDOR] — [Smartlead benchmarks](https://www.smartlead.ai/benchmarks); [Lemlist cold email benchmarks](https://lemlist.com/blog/cold-email-benchmarks/)
- Expandi H1 2025: cold email **5.1%**, LinkedIn DM **10.3%**. [VENDOR] — [Expandi, State of LI outreach H1 2025](https://expandi.io/blog/state-of-li-outreach-h1-2025/)
- Valley: warm/signal-based **15-45%** vs cold without context **<1%** (30M+ messages). [VENDOR] — [Valley](https://www.joinvalley.co/blog/linkedin-reply-rate-benchmarks-2026)
- Letterdrop: engaged accounts **>30%** reply vs **<5%** standard. Letterdrop also published a "Study: effect of LinkedIn activity on outbound response rate" (details not read: host blocked). [VENDOR] — [Letterdrop](https://letterdrop.com/blog/study-effect-of-linkedin-activity-on-outbound)
- Single-founder test: same template, **31%** reply from an active, posting profile vs **9%** from a dormant profile. [SELF-REPORTED, n=1] — [IndieHackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/my-cold-outreach-response-rate-jumped-from-8-to-31-heres-the-one-thing-i-changed-d6951a9357)
- Claim of **4.7x** reply when outreach references an engagement with content. No methodology. [VENDOR/consultancy] — [Koka Sexton, signal intelligence vs cold outreach](https://kokasexton.com/signal-intelligence-vs-cold-outreach/)
- Edelman-LinkedIn 2025: **91%** of hidden buyers say strong thought leadership makes them more receptive to the company's sales/marketing outreach (survey, declared attitude, not measured behavior). — [Edelman, hidden buyer](https://www.edelman.com/insights/hidden-buyer-b2b)
- Gartner 2025: **73%** of buyers actively avoid vendors with irrelevant outreach. This argues for contextual/warm outreach. — [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-sales-survey-finds-61-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-buying-experience)

### Inferences
- Content and outbound look complementary: content creates familiarity and a signal (who engages); outbound captures the 5% who are in-market without waiting for them to show up. The "familiarity effect" is consistent across all sources (survey + vendor + anecdote), but its size (2x vs 10x) is uncertain.
- Recommended in-house test: message post engagers and a matched group of non-engagers with the same template, then compare reply and call-booked rates. (Planoly and other sources make the same suggestion.)

### Gaps
- No controlled or academic study of the causal effect of founder content on cold reply rates.
- All the warm-outbound figures come from tool vendors (Valley, Letterdrop, HeyReach, PhantomBuster, Expandi).

## 5. Agency case studies with numbers (mostly self-reported)

### Takeaway
Case studies with reliable numbers (MRR + leads/month + source) are scarce. What exists is self-reported, often from creators who sell courses or communities, so the numbers are marketing for their own offer. The best-documented one (Unify, $15M pipeline) is about a SaaS company, not an agency.

### Cited Findings
- Unify (B2B SaaS, not an agency): "Founder-led content: how we drove **$15M in pipeline** via LinkedIn". [SELF-REPORTED by the company] — [Unify](https://www.unifygtm.com/explore/founder-led-content-how-we-drove-15m-in-pipeline-via-linkedin)
- LinkedIn-focused agency (HubSpot blog): **£2M ($2.6M)** of revenue in the first year, all inbound from LinkedIn, no sales team. [SELF-REPORTED, guest post] — [HubSpot blog](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/linkedin-selling-and-authenticity)
- Optimist (content agency, Tyler Hakes): steady inbound from consistent LinkedIn commenting and posting; **$1.5M** of revenue in 3 years. [SELF-REPORTED] — [Pilot, Tyler Hakes](https://pilot.com/blog/tyler-hakes-on-building-a-founder-brand)
- RevenueZen (Alex Boyd): one personal LinkedIn account contributes a large share of pipeline and revenue (no figure in the excerpt). [SELF-REPORTED] — [Databox, Metrics & Chill RevenueZen](https://databox.com/metrics-and-chill-revenuezen)
- Manufacturing-automation AI agency: **4-6 inbound leads/month**, mix **60% referral / 30% inbound / 10% outbound**, inbound driven by LinkedIn case studies. [SELF-REPORTED, newsletter] — [AI Insiders HQ](https://aiinsidershq.beehiiv.com/p/meet-marcus-he-makes-72k-month-with-ai)
- AI agency YouTube channel (Jannis Moore, per a third-party summary): one video brought **21 potential clients**; "$110,000 in leads" is lead value, not revenue. [SELF-REPORTED, AI summary] — [Chaindesk summary](https://www.chaindesk.ai/tools/youtube-summarizer/how-to-generate-110-000-in-ai-agency-leads-in-7-days-gJLa3SV813M)
