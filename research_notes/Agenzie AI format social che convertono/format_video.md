# Video formats used by AI / AI-automation / software-dev agencies to attract clients

Research date: 2026-11-21. Method: web search only. Direct page fetches failed because the session's network blocks hosts such as captureflow.ai and atakinteractive.com, so most figures come from search-result extracts of the cited pages. Labels used below:
- **[VERIFIED]**: platform or third-party measured data.
- **[SELF-REPORTED]**: a claim by the creator or vendor.
- **[3RD-PARTY EST.]**: a blog or review estimate.
- **[INFERENCE]**: my own reasoning.

Subscriber counts move fast and the sources disagree, so treat every count as a snapshot.

## Q1. Which video formats recur, and which ones correlate with inbound leads (audience formats vs buyer formats)?

### Takeaway
The recurring buyer formats are long-form YouTube build tutorials and screen-shares, case-study or "real automation for a real business" walkthroughs, and how-to demos with a free template or lead magnet. Short-form clips, AI-news reactions and podcast clips mainly build reach. Measured, format-level lead attribution for AI agencies does not exist publicly. The evidence is anecdotes from creators, such as Liam Ottley getting paid calls from his first YouTube videos and Automatable clients saying they "found us through Jono's YouTube channel", plus general B2B data showing that how-to content holds attention best.

- **Audience vs buyer rule of thumb [INFERENCE]:** a format is a buyer format when the viewer it attracts holds the problem *and* the budget. In practice that means it is niche-specific (it names an industry or process), shows a business outcome in $ or hours, and ends with an audit or booking CTA. Build tutorials sit in between. They attract many builders who will never pay, but they prove competence to the few buyers who watch.
- **An important split in this niche [INFERENCE]:** most of the large "AI agency" YouTube channels (Ottley, Saraev, Herk, Catliff) now earn more from *teaching people to run agencies* (Skool, accelerators) than from agency clients. Their "conversion" evidence is mostly community sign-ups. An agency selling services to SMBs or enterprises should copy their production mechanics, not their topic selection.

### Gaps
- No public dataset attributes agency leads to a specific video format. All format-to-lead links are anecdotal or editorial.
- No public data exists for teardown/audit, whiteboard or live-build formats used by AI agencies specifically.
- I could not open the primary Wistia, Vidyard and HubSpot reports. The figures come via summaries, so check them before publishing.

## Q3. The YouTube long-form → agency client pipeline as described by these creators

### Takeaway
The described pipeline is a long-form tutorial or case study, then a description link to a free template, resource or Skool community, then a booking link for a discovery, audit or "walkthrough" call, then a paid exploration or discovery phase, then the build. Guides say inbound usually starts after 4–8 months of weekly publishing (an editorial claim, not data).

### Cited Findings
- Ottley's early pipeline was video → paid consulting calls ($4,300 from calls at about 10k subscribers) → a caller asks for the build → first Morningside project **[SELF-REPORTED]**. — [X](https://x.com/liamottley_/status/1986941199568257401)
- Morningside's later sales motion uses a paid exploration phase of 2–4 weeks with a $20k minimum before development. It signed $200k of deals in 7 days per the video title **[SELF-REPORTED]**. A 30-minute discovery call appears on the landing page. — [Recapio](https://recapio.com/digest/signing-200000-of-ai-agency-deals-in-7-days-by-liam-ottley)
- Automatable's booking offers a "full 60-minute walkthrough with Jono", which acts as a free consult that is really a sales call. — [ContentBuck](https://contentbuck.com/case-studies/jono-catliff-channel-analysis)
- Launch Blitz recommends:
  - pairing key videos with downloadable checklists and templates
  - tracking opt-ins with UTMs and measuring YouTube-sourced booked calls
  - a **benchmark target of 1–3% conversion from views to lead magnet** **[EDITORIAL TARGET, not measured]**
  - — [Launch Blitz](https://www.launchblitz.co/learn/platforms/youtube-for-agency-owners)
- Suggested lead magnets include an "AI Readiness Checklist", "Digital Audit PDF" and "Strategy Template", with the booking link in both the description and the video. — [Digitaspro](https://www.digitaspro.com/how-to-attract-high-value-clients-using-youtube-in-2025/)
- Guides say meaningful inbound for AI-agency channels typically starts between month 4 and month 8 of consistent weekly or fortnightly publishing, and that retention is the key algorithm signal **[EDITORIAL]**. — [Tenacious Marketing](https://blog.tenaciousmarketing.co.uk/the-youtube-channel-launch-checklist-for-ai-agencies-how-to-build-trust-generate-inbound-leads-and-collapse-the-sales-funnel)
- An outside example of the same mechanic: a real-estate agent attributes $150M in sales over 5 years to one long-form video per week **[SELF-REPORTED, different industry]**. — [Inman](https://www.inman.com/2026/04/01/stop-chasing-leads-how-one-agent-built-a-150m-business-on-youtube/)

### Inferences
- **[INFERENCE]** "YouTube is my best sales rep" works because buyers pre-qualify themselves by watching hours of content. Ciela's unverified 14.6% vs 1.7% close-rate comparison points the same way. The CTA stack is usually: pinned comment plus description link to a free template, a Skool link, and a booking link for high-intent viewers.
- **[INFERENCE]** For a services agency that does not want to sell education, the variant is to keep the template CTA but make the free asset a niche audit or checklist rather than a DIY workflow, so it filters for buyers rather than builders.

### Gaps
- No creator publishes measured funnel numbers (views → template downloads → calls → closed deals). The phrase "YouTube is my best sales rep" could not be traced to a primary source.

## Q4. B2B video performance data (LinkedIn 2025–2026, Wistia, Vidyard, HubSpot, watch time, short vs long)

### Takeaway
LinkedIn reports roughly 36% year-over-year growth in video viewing or watch time (sources disagree on the period, 2024 vs Feb 2024–Feb 2025 vs 2025) and about 27% growth in video creation. Native video engagement sits around 5–6%, which is below documents and carousels on LinkedIn. Across B2B datasets, short videos hold attention best, but how-to content holds up even at longer lengths.

## Q5. Production: effort, AI tools, and whether AI-avatar content performs

### Takeaway
AI use in video production more than doubled year over year (Wistia 2025), and AI avatars are spreading fast (Vidyard highlights "explosive adoption"). However, no independent evidence shows that avatar videos outperform human-presenter videos, and HeyGen's own survey says trust depends on disclosure. The reference creators who win clients appear on camera themselves.

### Cited Findings
- Wistia 2025: "use of AI in video production more than doubled over last year". — [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/wistias-2025-state-of-video-report-shows-use-of-ai-in-video-production-more-than-doubled-over-last-year-302411243.html)
- Vidyard 2025 highlights "explosive adoption" of AI avatars. Secondary sites cite 12x growth in 8 months **[unverified]**. Vidyard's own Video Agent test cut meeting no-shows from 13% to 9% **[SELF-REPORTED internal]**. — [Vidyard](https://www.vidyard.com/business-video-benchmarks/); [Teleprompter](https://www.teleprompter.com/blog/video-marketing-statistics)
- HeyGen "State of AI Avatars 2026": the top factor that would make respondents trust a brand's avatar video more was "transparent about AI use" (39.9%) **[VENDOR SURVEY]**. — [HeyGen](https://www.heygen.com/the-state-of-ai-avatars-2026)
- HeyGen-attributed claims **[VENDOR / ANECDOTE, no human-video control]**:
  - Avatar videos get about 35% higher email opens than text.
  - Reply rates rose from 5% to 15% in an unnamed outreach case.
  - — [AI Smart Ventures](https://aismartventures.com/posts/heygen-for-sales-outreach-ai-avatars-without-fake-looks/)
- A B2B agency uses a HeyGen clone of a spokesperson in B2B ads. This is a use case, not a performance study. — [Kalungi](https://www.kalungi.com/blog/heygen-ai-avatar-clone-executive-b2b-ads)
- A founder can record 4–6 talking-head LinkedIn videos in one 2-hour session (production cadence advice). — [Influencers-Time](https://www.influencers-time.com/linkedin-native-video-a-brand-thought-leadership-playbook/)
- Ben AI publicly discusses building "AI influencer clones" (e.g. a WhatsApp clone build video), which shows avatars used as *content subject matter* in demos. — [videohighlight](https://videohighlight.com/v/0Ad73-yaV-k)

### Inferences
- **[INFERENCE]** A typical production stack in this niche:
